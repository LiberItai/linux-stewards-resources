# Remote IT support: preparation checklist

A customer-first checklist to get a Windows, Mac or Linux problem ready for remote
help, so the first conversation is focused and nothing important is missed.

Work through the sections below before you contact Linux Stewards (or any support
provider). You do not need to answer everything — write "don't know" where you are
unsure.

## 1. The problem, plainly

- [ ] **Symptoms** — what is going wrong, in your own words, and what you expected to happen instead.
- [ ] **Device and operating system** — computer, laptop, phone or server; Windows, macOS or Linux; version if you know it.
- [ ] **When it started** — the date and time you first noticed it, and whether it was working before.
- [ ] **Recent changes** — any update, new install, setting change or new device around the time it started (it is fine to say "none that I know of").

## 2. The evidence

- [ ] **Exact error message** — copy the wording word-for-word, or note that there is none.
- [ ] **Screenshot** — take one if it helps; crop or blur out anything sensitive (see section 6).

## 3. Who and what is affected

- [ ] **How many users and devices** — is it one person, one machine, or everyone?
- [ ] **Business impact** — what work is stopped or slowed while the problem continues.

## 4. Safety before anyone changes anything

- [ ] **Backups** — are the important files backed up, and have you confirmed that a backup can actually be opened?
- [ ] **Remote-access constraints** — can the device reach the internet, and are you able to share the screen or run commands with the technician?
- [ ] **Authorisation** — who is allowed to approve changes, and who holds any account or administrator passwords?

## 5. What "done" looks like

- [ ] **Success criteria** — describe what a fixed result looks like, so the scope is clear.
- [ ] **Scope and price** — agree what will be done and the price before work starts.

## 6. Sensitive information

Keep passwords, recovery codes, private keys, account numbers and other people's
private messages out of any form or screenshot. Describe an account-access problem
without sharing the secret itself.
